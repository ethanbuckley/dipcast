About these forecasts

For each spot, Dipspot traces the river upstream, finds every monitored storm overflow whose water reaches it, and combines what those overflows are doing now with how likely each is to spill over the next five days, allowing for travel time, bacterial die-off and dilution. It is a forecast, not a water test: "low" is not a guarantee of clean water, and inclusion of a spot is not a statement that it is safe to swim there. How accurate is it?

What the levels mean

Each level is a band of the day's exposure index (0–100): the modelled chance that a spill from at least one upstream overflow reaches the spot.

Low under 15
A spill reaching the spot is unlikely. Rain still washes in runoff from farms, roads and wildlife, which the index does not cover.
Moderate 15 to 40
Some chance that sewage from an overflow upstream reaches the spot.
High 40 to 70
A spill reaching the spot is more likely than not to be close: usually several overflows upstream are expected to spill.
Very high 70 or more
A spill reaching the spot is likely.
No overflows or Not covered
No monitored overflow upstream, or no river connection the model can trace. There is nothing for the forecast to flag, which is not the same as clean water.
What each part of a spot's page means
Right now
Which upstream storm overflows are discharging now or finished within 48 hours, from the water companies' live feeds.
Exposure index (0–100)
The modelled chance that at least one upstream overflow spills and its discharge reaches the spot after travel time, die-off and dilution. The spill part is verified against what the overflows did; the die-off and dilution weights are physical estimates, not calibrated against water samples, so it is an index, not a probability. The colour levels (low to very high) come from it.
E. coli > 900
The estimated probability that a midday water sample would exceed 900 E. coli cfu/100 ml, from rainfall at the spot, the exposure index, water-body type and season, fitted to Environment Agency samples at river and lake bathing waters and tested on years and sites it never saw. It counts bacteria from any source, so rain alone can raise it when the exposure index is low. 900 is the threshold used in the inland "sufficient" classification, which is a 90th-percentile calculation over four seasons, not a pass/fail test of one sample. It is validated on rivers in the May–September sampling season only; on lakes it has no demonstrated skill and is not shown.
Algae
Designated bathing waters only: the Environment Agency sampler's latest look at the water, an observation, not a forecast. It does not tell blue-green algae from harmless kinds.
What it does not know
Anything about a specific farm, road, outfall or algal bloom: only monitored storm overflows are modelled.
Saved spots, offline use and your data

Saved spots are kept in this browser only, not sent anywhere. After a first visit the page and the latest forecast are stored on your device, so the site opens without signal at the water's edge; it then says how old the forecast is. Privacy notice.
