dipcast

Will sewage from upstream storm overflows reach this swim spot?

Three different things are shown. Observed: which upstream storm overflows are discharging now or finished within 48 h, from the water companies' live feeds. Exposure index (0–100): the modelled chance that at least one upstream overflow spills and its discharge reaches the spot after travel time, die-off and dilution. The spill part is verified against what the overflows did; the die-off and dilution weights are physical estimates, not calibrated against water samples, so it is an index, not a probability. E. coli > 900: the estimated probability that a midday water sample would exceed 900 E. coli cfu/100 ml, from rainfall at the spot, the exposure index, water-body type and season, fitted to Environment Agency samples at river and lake bathing waters and tested on years and sites it never saw. 900 is the threshold used in the inland "sufficient" classification, which is a 90th-percentile calculation over four seasons, not a pass/fail test of one sample. It is validated on rivers in the May–September sampling season only; on lakes it has no demonstrated skill and is not shown. Forecasts, not measurements: the model knows nothing about a specific farm, outfall or algal bloom, and "low" is not a guarantee of clean water. Inclusion of a spot is not a statement that it is safe to swim there. How accurate is it?
