About SwimSignal

SwimSignal is a free five-day pollution risk forecast for river and lake swim spots in England. It covers 89 spots, 38 of them designated bathing waters. It uses the water companies' live storm-overflow data, rainfall forecasts and the river network. It is a forecast, not a water test.

How it works

For each spot, SwimSignal traces the river network upstream and finds every monitored storm overflow up to 60 km upstream along it. It takes what those overflows are doing now from the water companies' live feeds. It forecasts how likely each one is to spill on each of the five days, from the rain forecast and the overflow's own spill record. It then allows for how long sewage takes to travel downstream, how fast bacteria die off and how much the river dilutes it, so an overflow 2 km upstream counts for more than one 40 km up a tributary. Its spill forecast for each day is the exposure index, a 0–100 score of how likely sewage from upstream is to reach the spot. On rivers it also gives an E. coli estimate: the chance that a water sample would fail the E. coli limit for bathing water, and at designated bathing waters it adds the Environment Agency's rating and its sampler's latest algae check. A day's level (low, moderate, high or very high) is the worst of these: the exposure index, the E. coli estimate from May to September, a "poor" rating and an algae check from the last 14 days.

Overflows are listed under the names their water companies give them, which use abbreviations: WwTW and STW are a wastewater (sewage) treatment works, as is WRC, a water recycling centre; CSO is a combined sewer overflow and SSO a storm sewage overflow; PS is a pumping station and SPS a sewage pumping station.

What it cannot see

  • The spill forecast covers monitored storm overflows only. The E. coli estimate allows for rain washing in pollution from other sources on average, but knows nothing about a particular farm, road, boat, misconnected drain or incident.
  • Dŵr Cymru Welsh Water publishes no live overflow status. Its overflows, which matter at the spots on the Dee and the Wye, are forecast from rain and their yearly spill record, with no "right now".
  • It does not show the Environment Agency's short-notice advice against bathing after pollution or an algal bloom.
  • It says nothing about currents, cold water or other dangers.
  • A "low" reading means nothing the site looks at flags the day. It does not mean the water is clean, or that it is safe to swim there.

How good it is

Forecasts are scored afterwards against what the overflows actually did and against the Environment Agency's water samples. The results are published on the accuracy page, including where the forecast was wrong. The E. coli estimate is tested on rivers in the May to September sampling season only. Please take any figures from the accuracy page: they change as new results come in.

Who runs it

SwimSignal is a free, non-commercial project run by one person, Ethan Buckley, not a company. It is not run by, connected with or endorsed by the Environment Agency, any water company, Water UK or the Met Office. The code is open source under the MIT licence. See the terms of use.

Help test it this winter

From October 2026 to February 2027 a small group of swimmers and river groups are trying SwimSignal and reporting what helps and what is wrong. If you are one of them, or would like to be, the testing page says what the site claims, what it does not, what to look for and how to tell us.

Contact

Email hello@swimsignal.co.uk. To report a problem or ask for a spot to be added, you can also use the feedback form or the spot request form. These prepare a private email for you to review and send; no GitHub account is needed.

Data

Storm overflow data from the water companies via the National Storm Overflow Hub, and data from the Environment Agency, Ordnance Survey, Open-Meteo, the Met Office and OpenStreetMap. Sources, credits and licences.

The files the site is built from are public too: every spot's forecast, the storm overflows and the accuracy scores. Data files lists each one, what it holds and its licence.

Show a spot's forecast on your site

A club, a council or anyone else can show one spot's forecast on their own web page: its headline, its five days, the caveat, when it was issued, a link to the full forecast and the data credits. It updates with each forecast. Paste this into the page, with the spot's id in place of wharfe-burnsall:

<iframe src="https://swimsignal.co.uk/embed.html?spot=wharfe-burnsall" title="Pollution risk forecast from SwimSignal" width="100%" height="640" style="border:0; max-width:480px" loading="lazy"></iframe>

A spot's id is the last part of its page's address: swimsignal.co.uk/spot/wharfe-burnsall/ is wharfe-burnsall. The card fits a column from 320 pixels wide. On 3 October 2026 the tallest card was 638 pixels high in a column 320 pixels wide, and 488 at 480 pixels; a card taller than its frame scrolls inside it. See the card for River Wharfe, Burnsall.

The terms of use allow this as long as the card is shown whole: with its caveat, its credits and its link back to SwimSignal.
