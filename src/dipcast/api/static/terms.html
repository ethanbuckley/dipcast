What Dipspot is

Dipspot is an experimental tool. It estimates the chance that sewage from storm overflows upstream affects a spot on a river or lake in England, now and over the next few days. On rivers it also estimates the chance that a water sample would fail the E. coli limit for bathing water. It combines the water companies' published overflow data, rainfall forecasts, the river network and statistical models. At designated bathing waters it repeats the Environment Agency's yearly rating and its sampler's latest algae check. It is a forecast, not a measurement.

What it is not

Dipspot does not test water, and it cannot tell you whether it is safe to swim. Its spill forecast covers monitored storm overflows only. Its E. coli estimate allows for rain washing in pollution from other sources on average, but knows nothing about a particular farm, road, boat, outfall or incident. The algae check is the Environment Agency's observation on the date shown, and it does not tell harmful algae from harmless kinds. The site does not show advice against bathing given at short notice, after pollution or an algal bloom. A "low" reading means nothing the site looks at flags the day. It does not mean the water is clean or safe, and it says nothing about currents, cold water or other dangers. A "high" reading is a probability, not a confirmed spill.

Before you swim

  • Nothing on this site is advice about your health or safety. Whether to go in the water is your decision.
  • Do not rely on Dipspot alone. Check the signs at the water. At a designated bathing water, check the Environment Agency's page for it too: it shows advice against bathing that Dipspot does not. Each bathing water's page on Dipspot links to it.
  • Open water has dangers the site does not cover, among them cold water, currents, weirs, depth, hidden objects and algae.
  • If someone is in trouble in the water, call 999. If you are unwell after swimming, contact your GP or NHS 111 and say that you have been swimming.

Accuracy

Everything on the site is given without responsibility for its accuracy. How often the forecast is right is published on the accuracy page, including where it is wrong. Overflow status can be late, missing or wrong at source; some overflows have no live monitor; rainfall forecasts have errors that grow with lead time; the river network and outfall positions are approximate. Expect the forecast to be wrong some of the time.

Water companies' data

Each overflow's status is shown as its water company publishes it. Whether an overflow is "expected to spill" is Dipspot's own model estimate, not a statement by the company, and it does not mean the company has broken any rule.
