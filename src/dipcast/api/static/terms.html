Terms of use

Plain-English terms for using dipcast. Last updated 12 September 2026.

What dipcast is

dipcast is a free, experimental tool that estimates the chance that sewage from upstream storm overflows affects a point on a river or lake in England, now and over the coming days. It combines water companies' published overflow data, rainfall forecasts, river-network geometry and a statistical model. It is a forecast, not a measurement.

What it is not

dipcast does not test water. It does not know about pollution from farms, roads, boats, wildlife, treated sewage effluent, industrial discharges, algae or anything other than monitored storm overflows. A "low" reading means the model sees little risk from monitored overflows; it does not mean the water is clean or safe. A "high" reading is a probability, not a confirmed spill.

Nothing on this site is advice about your health or safety. Whether to enter the water is your decision. If you are unwell after swimming, seek medical advice. For official water-quality information use the Environment Agency's bathing-water service and any local notices.

Accuracy

The model's skill is published on the verification page, including where it is wrong. Overflow status can be delayed, missing or incorrect at source; some overflows have no live monitor; rainfall forecasts have error that grows with lead time; the river network and outfall positions are approximate. Expect the forecast to be wrong some of the time.
